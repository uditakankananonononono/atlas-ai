# E9 v2: cognitive multiverse — population of minds, counterfactual-credit selection vs best-score

Label: island-model quality-diversity / novelty search with credit assignment, in the family of MAP-Elites (Mouret & Clune 2015, arXiv:1504.04909), novelty search (Lehman & Stanley, Evol. Comput. 19(2) 2011), POET (Wang et al. 2019, arXiv:1901.01753) and FunSearch's island populations (Romera-Paredes et al., Nature 2023). No novelty claim. Source pin: atlas-ai main 4f68f54a (which contains the e6/e7/e8 harnesses). Built on `experiments/e7_library_transfer` (same PRIMS, apply_prog/verify, budget unit = programs evaluated, max_depth 4, train(8)/test(8) accept-then-verify). A bfs+short mind reproduces e7's solve() exactly (same program, same evaluation count) on all 40 tasks x 4 budgets = 160 cases (test_multiverse.py::test_bfs_short_matches_e7_solve). All numbers below are from a full suite rerun on Python 3.12.14 (pytest 9.1.1, numpy 2.5.4), 10 seeds main + 6 seeds spammer, deterministic under sha256-derived int seeds with a cross-process (two PYTHONHASHSEEDs) determinism test.

Supersedes v1: the v1 delivery seeded `random.Random` with tuples, which is rejected on Python 3.12 and makes every result depend on PYTHONHASHSEED and worker-process assignment; the v1 numbers (including its paired CIs and its spammer-survival gap) do not reproduce and are withdrawn.

Setup: 8 minds over e7's 40 authored pipeline tasks, heterogeneous in strategy (bfs / beam / rand), per-task budget (1000 / 300, e7's budgets), episodic memory, tiebreak objective, representation (2 dropped list prims + personal macro library, cap 8). Generations of 8 fresh training tasks; verified solves invent length-2/3 macro windows published to a shared pool with lineage provenance; usage-weighted adoption; selection every 3 generations keeps 6 of 8; offspring inherit the parent's lineage and library with mutation, 15% fresh lineage. Held-out: h2 = a whole e7 domain left out (fold = seed mod 4; all folds covered across seeds 1..10), h1 = 10 held-out training-domain pipelines; identical fresh eval draws per seed across arms (paired, and now process-independent). Arms: iso-best (no sharing), share-rand, share-best, share-multi (usage credit), share-multi-v2 (counterfactual credit: on each solve using another lineage's macro, the solver re-attempts the task with that macro removed from library and cache at the same budget; the inventor's lineage is credited only if the solve then FAILS), e7-lib (e7's validation-gated learn, 1 agent), e7-lib-8 (8 independent e7 libraries = equal attempts per task with the population arms). Selection value: own window solves + 1.0 x window credit. Spammer suite: one junk-publishing mind, counterfactual vs publication credit. All data synthetic and authored (e7's suite).

## What failure showed
1. The direction fails twice: neither usage credit (v1) nor counterfactual credit (v2) makes selection beat plain best-score on held-out transfer. The bottleneck is not the credit metric.
2. Better attribution is real but insufficient: counterfactual credit more than doubles the fraction of credits that correspond to positive true marginal (38% vs 15%), yet outcomes are unchanged (7/47 v2 credits are still on lineages with negative true marginal, vs 6/52 in v1 - no reduction there). Selection-level credit assignment is not where the transfer value is lost.
3. Solve-time necessity still is not population-level marginal contribution: 7/47 v2-credited lineages hurt the population when their macros are held (bloat under fixed budget). A fully honest credit would need the ablation itself, which costs a re-evaluation per lineage.
4. The strongest robust effect is boring: sharing macros raises the average mind on familiar pipelines (+0.207 h1_ind) but buys nothing on the genuinely held-out domain once attempts are equalized, and 8 independent e7 libraries match or beat every population arm per mind (e7-lib-8 h2_ind 0.596 vs best 0.537).

## Follow-ups (not done)
- Selection on ablation-computed marginal (expensive) or dropping selection entirely: e7-lib-8 suggests N independent validation-gated libraries + any-of voting is the strong baseline to beat.
- Adoption gating: adopt only macros that raise the adopting mind's own validation solves (per-mind e7 rule), which directly attacks the library bloat measured by the macro-removal ablation.
- lam/population-size sweeps are unjustified until an arm beats e7-lib-8 on h2_ind with CI excluding 0.
